The wowdps window, reworked

An interactive prototype for the windowed GUI, built from Sunday night's Heroic Coiled Altar kill: 25 players, six deaths, one Heroism. Every row, curve and cooldown in it comes from your own log and history store. The critique that drove it and a build plan for the Rust side follow below.

What's wrong today

Taken from the release build running against the live daemon, windowed at 1440 by 1000 on a headless output, walking every screen on tonight's log and the 801-card history store. The overlay was not reviewed. Ranked by how much each problem costs you after a pull.

1The meter starts 40% of the way down the window

Above the first row sit the tab strip, a title line, the instance strip, a second title line with ‹ › that repeats the name and KILL, two stat cards 110 px tall, the view tabs, an always-on filter box and the column heads. That's about 380 px of a 1000 px window. On a 25-player raid, 14 rows fit.

Prototype: one title line with a stat line under it, the ribbon, then tabs. The first row starts near 215 px and 21 rows fit. The filter shrinks into the tab row and grows when you press /.

GUI only

2One fight lives in two places, and four controls move between pulls

Tonight's pulls are under "fights", a list titled WoWCombatLog-092726_185854.txt. Older pulls are under "history", with no dates, and they open in a second renderer (history::stored_screen) that has no graph, deaths, mitigation, compare or strip. Strip discs, the ‹ › chips, [ ] and the fights tab all change the pull, and each walks a different scope: visit, visit members, whole log, list.

Prototype: one pull rail. Tonight is on top, earlier nights follow from the store, and every row has the same shape. Rows are grouped by night and visit, and your character's colour marks who played. [ ] walk the rail. A stored pull opens in the same workspace as a live one.

GUI only SegmentList.log_id plus a row's start_ms already name its stored fight id, so the two lists merge without duplicates.

3Drilling in throws the meter away

To look at a second player you back out, move and drill again. At 1440 px a player's name and their numbers sit 1,000 px apart, while the drill has no player header at all: no spec, rank, dps or deaths.

Prototype: master and detail. The meter keeps a sensible measure, and the selected player's drill sits beside it. j k update the inspector as you move. Below 820 px it falls back to today's push-and-back.

GUI only A Watch with drill set already returns the rows and the breakdown in one snapshot. Only ClientState has to stop treating a drill as a screen change.

4The accent is loud, unstable, and says the wrong thing

Death Knight crimson fills the headline card, the active tab and the title. "Raid dps" and "deaths 6" read as alarms, and the colour collides with WIPE red and with every DK's bar. The window starts in neutral blue and turns crimson only once Home resolves an owner. If a pull is live at launch it can stay blue all session. And the lock pins Tränq while tonight was played on Tranqlock, so Home opened after the raid shows a stale week.

Prototype: gold is the interface, as in the game's own frames. Class colours mark people, and that includes you: your row, your chip, your name in the top bar, in whichever character you're playing. Green and red only mean outcomes. The class-coloured chrome survives as an option (the Chrome switch above) and is resolved at start-up from config, never after a query lands.

GUI only

5Graphs have no axes, and spans flood them

Cooldown, external and mitigation windows are drawn as full-height tinted bands. On a tank's Taken drill they overlap into a red wash that hides the curve. There is no time axis and no scale, and the legend's "mitigation" and "defensive" are two near-identical salmons.

Prototype: the curve has the plot to itself, with minute ticks and a peak label. Spans move to labelled lanes under it (cooldowns, items, externals, defensives), and every span wears its caster's class colour. An external tells you who gave it: Heroism in Shaman blue, Rallying Cry in Warrior tan, Power Word: Barrier in Priest white.

GUI only Marks already carry caster.

6Deaths are sorted by count, and the recap has no shape

Six players with one death each draw six identical full-width bars. The order of deaths is the thing a raid leader needs, and it isn't there. The recap is a list of realm-suffixed names with hit points as a bare percentage, and it doesn't show time before death.

Prototype: deaths in the order they happened, each with its time, killing blow, source and overkill. The ribbon puts a skull at each one. The recap draws a health bar per event and calls out damage you did to yourself: your own Burning Rush took 30,660 of Tranqlock's last 129,903.

Wire change The meter's Deaths rows need each death's time and killing blow. Recap entries need an offset before death to draw a time axis.

7Home leads with a vanity number

The largest element is "pulls 47". The panels sit in a grid that leaves holes, and every figure is for the locked character only. The first question after a raid is "how did last night go for me", and Home can't answer it if last night was on an alt.

Prototype: Home opens on last night: the pulls you did, your rank on each, and how it moved across the night. Under that are keys against their timers (with the +1, +2 and +3 marks), raid progress with one dot per pull, and your key throughput across characters. Scope is a chip row (all characters, or one), not a lock.

GUI only Card me blocks carry rank, count and par_ms today.

8Yellow means five things, and you aren't marked at all

Yellow is LIVE, crit %, stagger, the Σ visit title and a wipe's best %. Semantic colour should be scarce, so each use keeps its meaning. Meanwhile Tranqlock's own row looks like everyone else's, and meter_stats matches the owner by label.

Prototype: live is a pulsing dot. Crit is plain ink. A wipe's best % sits beside its red cross. Your row carries a "you" tag and your rank in your class colour. The header states your place: 17th of 19 dps.

Wire change A per-row mine flag from the daemon's own-character set (addon plus configured characters), so every one of your characters is "you" without matching labels.

9Two type voices and system words

Names are in Noto Sans, while numbers and column heads are in a monospace. That reads as a terminal pasted into a window. Copy speaks the system's language: a log file name as a title, "accents optional" in the filter, "kept from retention", "2/7" for a position, "graph: dps".

Prototype: one semi-condensed family with tabular figures does both names and numbers. Marcellus appears only for encounter names, a nod to the game's Friz Quadrata. Labels say what things are: "Tonight", "Pull 1 of 1", "Filter players".

GUI only Two OFL fonts bundled with include_bytes!. See Tokens.

10Smaller defects found on the way

  • hide_realms is ignored in drill panes and the recap: "Bearlysimpin-Proudmoore-US" is truncated mid-realm.
  • On Home, j k Enter [ ] d reach the hidden meter's ClientState, and t opens talents for the hidden meter's row.
  • m doesn't close History, though the live tab does. t is swallowed on History.
  • Esc doesn't close the ⚙ options card, and keys typed while it is open reach the meter.
  • Esc on the fight list opens Home and Esc on Home closes it, so Esc toggles rather than backs out.
  • The total row is pinned to the window's bottom edge, 600 px below a short list.
  • The Deaths drill shows DeathWindow.at_ms only when a player died twice or more.
  • A stored key's member bosses are unreachable: GetFight hard-codes boss: None.
  • The talent viewer shows 36/34 and 14/13 points with no warning colour.
  • The view tabs sit above the stat cards on a stored fight and below them on a live one.

Build plan

Six pull requests, ordered so each one ships something visible and none leaves the window half-converted. The wire changes land together as one protocol bump, because each bump renames the socket and restarts every client.

Constraints the Rust side must keep in mind. A canvas composites images above paths, so ability icons in the inspector stay widgets, not canvas images. text_input captures the press, so the palette's field uses the filter's release-to-focus pattern. iced has no sticky positioning, so the total row goes under the list when the list is short and pins when it overflows (measure with responsive). The overlay imports view.rs renderers, so the new ones stay message-generic.
